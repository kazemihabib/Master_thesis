\guide{The abstract is a self-contained miniature of the whole thesis. Cover, in
this order and in 1--2 sentences each: (1) the problem and why it matters;
(2) the gap or open question; (3) what you did (your approach in one line);
(4) how you evaluated it (datasets, models, metric); (5) your main quantitative
results; (6) the take-away conclusion. Avoid acronyms you do not define, avoid
references, and do not promise more than the thesis delivers.}

\guide{Example skeleton you can rewrite: ``Face-swapping techniques have become
realistic enough to threaten the reliability of visual media. This thesis
investigates whether multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can detect
face-swapped images in a zero-shot setting, without task-specific training.
We build an extensible evaluation pipeline that runs N open-weight MLLMs over
the FOWS and GOTCHA datasets under a range of prompting strategies, and measure
detection performance with accuracy and balanced metrics. We find that \dots.
These results suggest \dots.''}
