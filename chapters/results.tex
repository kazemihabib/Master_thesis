\chapter{Results and Discussion}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter presents the empirical evaluation and forensic analysis of multimodal large language models for single image face swap detection. Across all experiments, models are evaluated in off the shelf configurations without parameter fine tuning, measuring their intrinsic ability to identify facial manipulation artifacts under zero shot and few shot prompting regimes.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Overview and Experimental Roadmap}
\label{sec:results_overview}

To manage computational tractability, experiments follow an integrated two tier structure within each stage. Experiments are first systematically explored and filtered on balanced development sets from FOWS and GOTCHA. The optimal configurations identified in each stage are then validated on the complete benchmark test partitions, comprising over \num{80000} frames across both datasets.

\section{Stage 1: Zero Shot Lexical Search and Model Selection}
\label{sec:results_stage1}

Stage 1 evaluates the intrinsic zero shot capability of all nine candidate models across the complete $4 \times 7 = 28$ grid of real and fake target word pairs on the balanced development sets of FOWS and GOTCHA. The objective of this initial stage is threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Assess the baseline feasibility of zero shot face swap detection across diverse model architectures;
    \item Quantify model sensitivity to target class vocabulary words; and
    \item Identify the optimal word pair for each model and select the top five performers to advance to Stage~2.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Instruction Following and Parsing Reliability}
\label{subsec:stage1_reliability}

Across all nine models, 28 prompt word pairs, and two development sets, a total of \num{677376} individual image classifications were evaluated. Under the deterministic single phrase extraction policy ($\pi(t)$, \Cref{sec:formulation}), eight of the nine evaluated models achieved a \num{100}\% parse success rate with zero formatting errors. Across the entire evaluation, only a single query from Nemotron-Nano-12B failed to emit a clean binary verdict. The strict single phrase format constraint was followed reliably across all model architectures.

\subsection{Model Specific Vocabulary Preferences}
\label{subsec:stage1_vocab_preferences}

A central finding of Stage 1 is that \textbf{there is no universal optimal word pair} for zero shot face swap detection across multimodal architectures. As reported in \Cref{tab:results_stage1_winners}, every one of the top five advancing models achieves its highest MCC on a completely distinct vocabulary pair:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Qwen3.6-35B-A3B} performs best with \texttt{authentic} / \texttt{faceswapped};
    \item \textbf{Qwen3.6-27B} achieves its peak with \texttt{bona fide} / \texttt{faceswapped};
    \item \textbf{gemma-4-31B-it} prefers \texttt{bona fide} / \texttt{generated};
    \item \textbf{Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct} reaches its highest score with \texttt{original} / \texttt{synthetic}; and
    \item \textbf{Llama-4-Scout-17B} favors \texttt{authentic} / \texttt{fake}.
\end{itemize}

This divergence aligns with prior findings showing that multimodal models have distinct, model specific lexical sensitivities~\cite{ismithdeen2025promptception,dumpala2024sensitivity}. Evaluating all models with a single default pair (such as \texttt{real} / \texttt{fake}) would unfairly penalize models that prefer other words, underestimating their true detection ability.

The sensitivity analysis reveals two distinct behavioral regimes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High Capability Models Exhibit High Lexical Fragility:} The most capable models display the largest performance swings across word choices. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B exhibits the highest sensitivity ($\text{STD} = \num{0.130}$), with scores ranging from \num{0.097} on suboptimal pairs to \num{0.585} on its winning pair, a spread of \num{0.488} MCC driven entirely by changing two words in the prompt. Similarly, Kimi-VL, Qwen3.6-27B, and Llama-4-Scout show spreads exceeding \num{0.410} MCC.
    \item \textbf{Weaker Models Show Low Variance at Low Performance:} Models with the lowest standard deviations, such as GLM-4.6V-Flash ($\text{STD} = \num{0.043}$) and Phi-4-multimodal ($\text{STD} = \num{0.054}$), remain consistently near chance across all word pairs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Selection of Advancing Models}
\label{subsec:stage1_selection}

Based on the development set results, the five top ranking models (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Qwen3.6-27B, gemma-4-31B-it, Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct, and Llama-4-Scout-17B) are selected to advance to Stage~2. Each selected model is strictly locked to its own Stage~1 winning vocabulary pair (\Cref{tab:results_stage1_winners}) for all subsequent structural and persona evaluations.

The remaining four models (Nemotron-Nano-12B, GLM-4.6V-Flash, InternVL3.5-30B, and Phi-4-multimodal) achieved peak scores below \num{0.380} overall MCC on the development sets and were excluded from further exploration to maintain computational tractability.
